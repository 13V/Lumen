What we build

The hard parts, done properly.

Most app ideas are easy until they meet the real world — bad reception, a phone in a wet glove, an auditor asking for a record from March. That is the part we build for.

Location & geofencing

Core Location and MapKit done with a light touch — site boundaries, arrival checks and while-in-use permissions that pass review and do not flatten the battery.

Offline-first data

Core Data and SwiftData models that keep working in a basement car park, then reconcile cleanly when the signal comes back. No lost entries, no duplicate rows.

Documents & PDFs

Drawing sets, revisions and generated certificates with PDFKit — readable on a phone, shareable off it.

Camera & capture

Photo evidence attached to the right job, compressed sensibly and uploaded when there is bandwidth to spare.

Roles & permissions

Sign-in, org structure and per-role visibility, so the office and the crew see different things from the same data.

Push, deep links & widgets

APNs notifications that arrive when they matter and open the exact screen they refer to. Home Screen widgets and Live Activities where they earn their keep.

App Store release management

Provisioning, TestFlight builds, privacy nutrition labels, screenshots and review responses. We handle submission, including the rejections.

How it goes

Six stages, no mystery.

You see a build on your own phone early and often. Nothing is saved up for a big reveal at the end.

Discovery

1–2 weeks

We sit with the people who will actually use it and map the working day. You leave with a scope, a flow and a fixed build quote — yours to keep whether or not we continue.

Design

2–3 weeks

Screens designed against Apple’s Human Interface Guidelines, not a web layout squeezed onto a phone. Dynamic Type, VoiceOver and dark mode are decided here, not patched in later.

Build

6–12 weeks, typically

Swift and SwiftUI in two-week increments. A TestFlight build lands on your phone at the end of each one, so priorities can change while changing them is still cheap.

Field testing

2–3 weeks

Real users, real sites, real reception. This is where assumptions die — and where an app stops being a demo.

Release

1–2 weeks

App Store Connect, privacy labels, store listing and submission. We write the review notes and answer the reviewer, so a rejection costs you a few days rather than a launch.

Iterate

Ongoing

Crash monitoring, annual iOS releases, and the feature requests that only appear once people rely on it. Apps that stop being updated stop being used.

Stack

Apple’s frameworks, not a cross-platform compromise.

Questions

Before you ask.

What does an iOS app actually cost?

It depends almost entirely on scope, so we do not publish a headline number that would be wrong for you. A discovery sprint gives you a fixed quote for the whole build before you commit to it — and that document is yours regardless of who builds the app.

How long from first conversation to the App Store?

For a focused first version, usually three to five months end to end. Discovery and design take three to five weeks, the build six to twelve, then field testing and review. Apple’s own review is typically 24–48 hours, but we budget for a rejection because they happen.

Why native Swift rather than React Native or Flutter?

Cross-platform makes sense when you need Android and iOS on one budget and the app is mostly forms and lists. It costs you when the app leans on the device — location, camera, background behaviour, widgets — or when you want new iOS features on release day.

If Android matters as much as iOS, we will say so and point you to a cross-platform team rather than sell you something we are not.

Do you build the backend too?

Yes. Most business apps need one, and an app built against a backend somebody else designed in isolation tends to show it. If you already have an API, we will work with it.

Who owns the code?

You do, on final payment — source, repository history and the App Store listing under your own developer account. We will set the account up with you if you do not have one.

Can you take over an app somebody else built?

Often. We start with a paid audit of the codebase and give you an honest read: what is salvageable, what needs replacing, and what it will cost either way. Occasionally the answer is that a rebuild is cheaper than the rescue, and we will tell you that.

Do you work outside Adelaide?

Yes, anywhere in Australia. Discovery works best in person, so for interstate projects we usually travel for that week and run the rest remotely.

Got an app in mind?

Tell us what your people do all day and where it falls apart. That is usually enough for a first conversation.
