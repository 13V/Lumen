What “native” buys you.

A native app is written in the platform’s own language against the platform’s own frameworks. It is not a website in a shell, and not a shared codebase compromising between two operating systems.

It feels like the phone

Real gestures, real scroll physics, real keyboard handling. Users cannot articulate why a wrapped app feels wrong, but they can feel it, and they delete it.

It reaches the hardware

Location, camera, secure enclave, background modes, widgets, Live Activities — available directly rather than through whatever a bridge library exposes this quarter.

It keeps up with iOS

Every September Apple ships a new iOS. Native apps adopt it on day one. Wrapped apps wait for the wrapper, and sometimes the wrapper never comes.

Capabilities

What we actually build.

Business apps, field apps and internal tools — the kind where somebody’s working day depends on it not falling over.

Location, maps and geofencing

Site boundaries, arrival and departure checks, and route context. We keep permissions to while-in-use wherever the job allows it, because background location is the fastest way to a battery complaint and a review rejection.

Offline-first sync

Sites have bad reception. The app has to work anyway. We model data so that entries made underground survive, reconcile predictably, and never silently overwrite somebody else’s work.

Documents and certificates

Versioned drawing sets, generated PDFs and compliance records that hold up when somebody asks for them a year later.

Photo and media capture

Evidence attached to the right record, compressed sensibly, queued and uploaded when there is bandwidth.

Accounts and roles

Organisations, crews and per-role visibility, with sign-in that does not make people reset their password every Monday.

Notifications that earn attention

Push via APNs, deep-linked to the exact record. We are deliberately conservative here: an app that over-notifies gets its notifications switched off, and then the urgent ones never arrive either.

Accessibility, properly

Dynamic Type so the text scales, VoiceOver labels that make sense, contrast that survives sunlight, and tap targets sized for a gloved hand. Decided during design, not retrofitted after a complaint.

Process

How a build runs.

Discovery

1–2 weeks

We spend time with the people who will use the app. Not a requirements workshop in a boardroom — the actual job, wherever it happens. You leave with a scope, screen flow and a fixed quote, and that document is yours whether or not we build it.

Interface design

2–3 weeks

Every screen designed to Apple’s conventions so the app is legible to anyone who already owns an iPhone. Dark mode, Dynamic Type and VoiceOver are settled here, where they are cheap.

Build

6–12 weeks, typically

Two-week increments in Swift and SwiftUI. At the end of each one, a TestFlight build lands on your phone. You can change your mind while changing it is still cheap.

Field testing

2–3 weeks

Real users, on real sites, with real reception. Every app we have built has changed during this stage, which is the point of having it.

Submission

1–2 weeks

App Store Connect setup, privacy nutrition labels, store listing, screenshots and review notes. We write the reviewer’s notes and answer their questions, so a rejection costs days instead of a launch date.

After launch

Ongoing

Crash monitoring, the annual iOS compatibility pass, and the features that only reveal themselves once people depend on the thing.

Questions

Common ones.

Will you build for Android as well?

Not in-house. If Android matters as much as iOS to you, a cross-platform team is a better fit and we will say so rather than take the work. Where iOS is the priority — which it usually is for Australian trade, health and professional-services businesses — native is the stronger choice.

Do we need our own Apple Developer account?

Yes, and it should be in your company’s name, not ours. It costs USD 99 a year. We will walk you through enrolment, including the D-U-N-S number Apple asks organisations for, and we publish under your account so the listing is never hostage to us.

What happens if Apple rejects the app?

We handle it. Most rejections are about permission strings, account deletion or sign-in requirements, and we design around the common ones from the start. Submission is budgeted with a rejection built in, so it is a scheduled inconvenience rather than a crisis.

Can you work with our existing backend?

Yes. If you have an API we will build against it, and tell you plainly if something in it is going to make the app worse. If you do not have one, we will build that too.

What does it cost to keep an app alive?

Budget for an annual compatibility pass around each September iOS release, plus your Apple Developer membership and whatever your backend hosting costs. Our Care Plan bundles the compatibility work with a monthly block of feature time.

Tell us about the job.

What your people do all day, and the part that keeps going wrong. That is enough to start.
